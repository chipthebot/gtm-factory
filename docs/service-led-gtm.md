# Service-Led GTM

The useful shift is simple: sell the finished work before selling the interface.

Many buyers do not want another tool to learn. They already pay for outcomes: leads, content, creative, research, launches, demos, enablement, campaigns, or partnerships.

A mission should look for a concrete job the buyer already needs done.

## Good Mission Shape

Strong missions have:

- a narrow buyer
- a clear usecase
- public signals that prove the usecase exists
- a small first deliverable
- a repeatable way to produce that deliverable
- a human review step before anything external happens

Weak missions sound like broad market research. They produce lists, but no clear reason to care.

## What To Sell First

Lead with a small finished outcome:

- a launch brief
- a usecase packet
- a creative audit
- a partner shortlist
- a content angle map
- a demo storyboard
- a campaign teardown
- a prospect-specific recommendation

The first deliverable should be concrete enough that a human can judge it in five minutes.

## Human Judgment

The factory can collect, summarize, draft, and compare. A human decides what is accurate, fair, useful, and worth reviewing.

Do not let throughput lower taste. A smaller batch with honest notes is better than a large batch of vague rows.
